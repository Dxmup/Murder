## V2M087: the primer

- **From:** The Metropolitan Review <weekend@metroreview.nyc>
- **Date shown:** Four days before the anniversary (Monday 5 April)
- **To:** everyone
- **Subject:** The weekend read: fifteen years of R, and the party nobody can agree about

THE WEEKEND READ

On Friday night about twenty people will ride a freight elevator to the fourth floor of 219 Harrow Street, the old Herald printing floor, put on masks, and raise a glass to a writer none of them can describe. Here is what to know before the arguments start.

WHO IS R?

For fifteen years, investigations have run under one letter. R began with street reporting from downtown blocks the city preferred not to look at, and never really left them: the Housing Authority, housing court, landlords, and the agencies that were supposed to be watching the landlords. The work was solid enough that editors printed it and careful enough that nobody has ever proved who wrote it. In fifteen years there has been no verified face, no legal name, no signed contract and no witnessed moment of writing.

The absence has become a genre of its own. Frankie Lowell, the retired Herald metro editor, has told the story of the first R letter arriving on the metro desk at more dinners than anyone can count. Newsrooms say "another R" when a caption claims more than its photograph shows. Colophon Press is putting together an anthology, The R Reader, with an introduction by the essayist Jules Kwan.

Ask around and you get three answers, usually loudly:

One person. A single reporter with good lawyers and better habits.

Several people. A name passed from hand to hand and kept consistent by discipline.

Nobody. A persona run by software, with people supplying legs and a voice when it needs them.

Each answer has believers on Friday's guest list. None of them has proof.

THE HOST

Veridian Dynamics is a small nonprofit that pays for investigative journalism no commercial newsroom will fund, keeps archives nobody else wants, and runs civic-technology projects. It is short of money, which is not a secret to anyone who has been asked for some. Friday is its anniversary fundraiser: drinks, remarks, an auction. Executive Director Arden Bell has promised nothing beyond that.

THE DONOR

Veridian's largest recent donor is Parallax Systems, the New York AI company founded by Dr. Nina Sen. The gift was announced as general support. Critics have pointed out the awkwardness of an AI company underwriting a party for a byline that some people believe is a machine.

THE RUMOR

Over the past six weeks, a story has moved through newsrooms, trading desks and group chats: that R, or someone close to R, has an investigation into Parallax ready to run. About two weeks ago "R may be looking at AI" hardened into "R has written an exposé." We could not find anyone who has read it. We could not find the first person who said it, either. Parallax has attracted short-sellers before, so some people stand to make money if the story is believed, and others stand to lose a great deal.

VALE

Vale is a reporter who has spent recent months asking people about R. Vale has told several of them, some of whom will be on the fourth floor on Friday, that an announcement is coming, and has said more than once that Vale knows what R is. Vale is on the guest list. Vale did not answer our messages.

THE RECKONING

Last Friday, an account long associated with R posted one line: the anniversary would include "a reckoning." The account is genuine. Who wrote the line, and what it means, nobody will say.

THE BLOCK

Harrow Street has its own claim on R. The building, the all-night counter on the corner and the tenants' association have been part of the legend for as long as there has been one, and the neighborhood has opinions about who gets to tell it. Kit Rakes, whose show The Rakes File has argued for years that "Who is R?" is too small a question, posted a Harrow Street teaser last week.

THREE QUESTIONS FOR FRIDAY

Who, or what, is R? What is Vale to R? And if an account is promising a reckoning, what is the party really for?

Bring a mask. Bring a theory. Expect to lose it.

## Review notes (not for players)

- Nothing above names a secret from any booklet. Checked against the audit of all 20 booklets and the pre-game mail.
- The primer quotes no R passage. Quoting early, middle and late R prose side by side would give everyone the material for the progressive ending.
- The three R theories get equal weight. The progressive theory is left out. It stays Kit's to pitch.
- The Night Desk is not linked to R. That link belongs to Tessa and would push people toward the Mantle ending.
- Graham appears as publicly short on Parallax before. There's no hint that Graham started the rumor.
- Ash and Dorian are left off. Both are switched Out.
