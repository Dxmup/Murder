# Casting by activity level

Updated 2026-09-17, for a sixteen-guest run (the sixteen core roles, no optional roles).

The number is **how hard the seat makes you work the room**, from 1 to 5. It is not how
important the character is: every core role carries evidence the endings need. A level 2 with
heavy inbound traffic is a busy, central evening spent standing still, which is the right seat
for someone who would hate chasing people across a room.

## Reading the levels

- **5. Drives the evening.** Cannot be played quietly. Three seats.
- **4. Constant negotiation** with a clear agenda and somewhere to stand between rounds.
- **3. Steady.** A handful of substantial conversations, mostly one to one.
- **2. Reactive and central.** People arrive with questions; you decide what to give them.
- **1. The easy-win seat.** Say little, be sought out late, still matter at the ballots.

## What this means for sixteen guests

There are only three genuinely low-effort seats in the core sixteen: Dee, Sam and Tony. The
other easy seats in the design (Farah and Manny) are optional roles, and optional roles are
deliberately built so they gate no ending, which is exactly why they are not in a sixteen-player
run. If more than three guests want to stand quietly at the edge, the fix is not to swap a core
role out. It is to pair a shy guest with Dee, Sam or Tony and cast the confident half of the
table into levels 4 and 5.

Kit is the one piece of casting that decides how the evening goes. Across fifteen simulation
runs, Kit's credibility arc determined on its own whether the progressive reading got anywhere.
Kit is also publicly disproven twice by design. Cast someone who can retract in front of people
and keep going.
