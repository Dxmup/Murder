# Mask shopping list

For a sixteen-guest run. Every specification here comes from a character's own
booklet, so what a guest reads and what they are handed agree.

Budget as listed is roughly **$150 to $190**. Two thirds of that is the three
expensive masks, which is the point: the money has to be visible from across the
room.

## Buy first: the three that cost real money

These three are the top of the class contrast. Buying them cheap collapses the
whole thing, because the argument only works if the gap is obvious at a glance.

| For | What to buy | Qty | Rough |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Jules Kwan** | A genuinely beautiful Venetian half-mask. Filigree metal or hand-painted papier-mâché, gold or deep colour, ribbon ties rather than elastic. The booklet says it cost more than Jules would admit. | 1 | $25–45 |
| **Graham Pike** | Absurd and expensive, worn as a joke that is not entirely a joke. Oversized gold animal, a full plague-doctor beak, something with horns. It should be the first mask anyone comments on. | 1 | $25–40 |
| **Celeste Park** | Elegant, and **on a handle**. A stick mask or lorgnette mask comes off in half a second when a camera appears, which is exactly what the booklet describes. If you cannot find one, buy an elegant half-mask and retie it on satin ribbon. | 1 | $15–25 |

## The plain and deliberate ones

Each of these is plain for a stated reason. Buy them flat, matte, and boring on
purpose.

| For | What to buy | Qty | Rough |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Arden Bell** | Plain black half-mask, good enough to be the host's own. Worn pushed up on the forehead more often than down. | 1 | $8 |
| **Dr. Nina Sen** | Matte black, minimal, no glitter, no shaping. Chosen so no photograph of Nina tonight is interesting. | 1 | $8 |
| **Sam Vale** | Plain, bare, black or white. It covers a face Sam does not want read. | 1 | $6 |
| **Tessa Quill** | Full coverage, featureless, chosen because it conceals rather than flatters. A plain white or black blank. **Get one with an open mouth** (see the warning below). | 1 | $10 |
| **Morgan Shaw** | Dark, three-quarter coverage, wide eye openings. Built for watching a room without being watched back. | 1 | $10 |

## The ones that must look cheap

Do not upgrade these. Dee's is borrowed and resented, Tony sells his by the
register, Rosa's is three years old, Eli never adjusted the one left in the box.
A matching set erases the story.

| For | What to buy | Qty | Rough |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Tony Calderón** | Flat paper masks on a thin elastic, the kind sold at a register. Buy the party pack. | pack of 12 | $8 |
| **Frankie Lowell** | A cheap plastic domino out of the door box. It should already be crooked. | from the box | — |
| **Eli Navarro** | Whatever is left in the box after everyone else has picked. Pull the ugliest one. | from the box | — |
| **Dee Nowak** | Borrowed at the door, ill-fitting, and it must sit close enough to the face to fog glasses. A snug elastic half-mask does this. | from the box | — |
| **Rosa Baptiste** | A plain cheap mask, visibly a few years old. Add a small printed sticker or ribbon so it reads as fundraiser merchandise the neighbourhood would recognise. | 1 + sticker | $5 |
| **Grace Okafor** | An unremarkable mask, plus **an enamel pin fixed to the temple**. The pin is the whole costume. Any union or political pin works. | 1 + pin | $12 |

## The door box

| What to buy | Qty | Rough |
|---|---|---|
| Plain half-masks, mixed black and white, elastic ties. Bulk packs are cheap. Assume a third of the room arrives without a mask, and Frankie, Eli and Dee all draw from this box by design. | 12–15 | $15–20 |

Put the box by the door, open, before the first guest arrives. Nobody should
have to choose between an unplanned costume and standing out.

## Two things to check before you buy

**Full-face masks muffle speech.** This is a three-hour party whose entire
mechanic is people talking to each other in a noisy room, and a sealed lower
face is a real problem for a player who has to negotiate all night. Tessa and
Morgan are the two with coverage written into their booklets. Buy theirs with an
open mouth or a cutaway chin, which keeps the concealment and returns the voice.

**Masks must not cover the name cards.** The cards are what make a masked room
playable. Nothing worn should hang over the chest.
